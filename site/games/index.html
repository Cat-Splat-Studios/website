Our games

What we're building,
and what we built

One game in active development, one shelved for now, and a shelf of prototypes that taught us how to make the first two.

In development

Sentinels of Gleamwood

A controller-first 2D action roguelite in the Dead Cells and Hades lineage. Built in Unity 6 for Windows, coming to Steam.

Steam page not open yet Wishlisting opens when the store page goes live.

A blade and a bow

You carry two weapons at a time and they do different jobs. The blade opens enemies up and puts them in the air. The bow is instant, so it is about timing a clean release rather than leading a shot.

The air is the point

Grounded hits build toward a launch, and once an enemy is up you have a fixed window to work with. The window never changes length, so the game rewards knowing exactly what you can fit inside it.

Finishers you have to set up

Every execution asks for a specific kind of hit against a specific state. Which ones you can reach depends on the two weapons you happen to be carrying, so a loadout is a set of options rather than a stat line.

Runs that carry forward

Dying ends the run but not the progress. You bank currency either way and spend it on movement and permanent upgrades, and the game builds levels around what you have bought.

The run

Ten biomes, and you will not see them all

A run is a handful of levels long. The first and the last never change, and everything between them is a web you route through yourself, picking a destination each time you leave a level.

One of the ways out skips the boss, its reward and the rest that follows, all at once. It is a trade rather than a shortcut, and it is usually the decision that shapes how a run ends.

Bosses

Three of them exist so far

Every biome ends in an arena and every arena is built, but only three of the fights are written. The rest are the next content job, along with the art.

The ones that do exist each teach something different: reading a wind-up, dealing with a room that keeps refilling, and a straight duel that changes its terms halfway through.

The writing

The story is not written yet

What is in the build today is a placeholder, and it will be replaced by people who write for a living. That is a deliberate order of work rather than a gap we are hoping to fill later.

Dialogue and story in Gleamwood are content, not code. A writer opens a file, types the lines, and the game reads them the next time it launches, with no programmer in the loop and no rebuild. Building that surface first is why the writing can arrive whenever it is ready without holding anything else up.

Also from the studio

A top-down arcade shooter in Unreal Engine 4, with 187 C++ files in the game module. Behaviour trees drive the enemies, with custom tasks, services and decorators, weight-based swarm evaluation and a shared target distributor. On top of that sit a combat director and an encounter subsystem that decide what shows up and when.

Shifter was a team project, and the parts we wrote are the player pawn and its controller, the AI movement component and controller, the combat director, and the encounter subsystem. Development is paused while Gleamwood has our attention.
